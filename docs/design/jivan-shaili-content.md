## Deliberate content gaps

1. **Languages:** all 28 state language lists use the user's regional-language table. Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh have separately requested, reference-based regional language examples. The UI identifies these as regional languages and varieties and explains that the lists are not exhaustive. Native language spellings remain absent until supplied with a source.
2. **Attire photographs:** no original individual state photographs were found among the repository's exhibition, gallery and design assets. The user then requested generated images in response to this gap. The individual assets in `public/attire/` are new, explicitly labelled AI illustrations based on the poster's pairings, not recovered originals. The poster's approximately 160 × 240-pixel people were not enlarged into the image panel, and unrelated cultural-programme photographs were not relabelled as these state pairs.
3. **Gujarati garment/community captions:** these are present but too small for confident complete transcription in the supplied screenshot. The code retains explicit nullable Gujarati fields. Request the source artwork or text; do not silently translate the English labels and present that translation as the poster's wording.
4. **Union territories:** both the poster and subsequent language table have 28 state entries and no union-territory entries. The user later requested new Bhusha illustrations and Bhasha content for Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. Their attire and regional language examples are separately researched; they have no invented poster numbers or Gujarati translations. The other six union territories retain the unavailable-content panel.
5. **Longer Bhajan explanation:** none appears in the provided materials. The current short explanation is grounded in the section takeaway. Replace it with supplied exhibition panel copy when available.

The Gujarati state names and the gender labels are accessible text, not image text. The original image remains unchanged; small temporary source-region views were used only for inspection, not copied into the application or enlarged as visitor-facing images.

## Generated attire artwork

The built-in `image_gen` tool produced one separate full-length paired portrait per state. Each prompt referenced the exact numbered poster entry, its garment names, palette and community identification, with warm ivory surroundings and complete headwear and footwear. Images are delivered as optimized WebP, at least 960 × 1440 pixels, and rendered using `object-fit: contain`. The page and `/references#jivan-shaili-credits` identify them as generated illustrations. Their cultural specifics still warrant curatorial review before public exhibition use.

Exact prompts and generation provenance are recorded in `docs/design/attire-generated-1-14.json` and `docs/design/attire-generated-15-28.json`. `node scripts/prepare-attire-manifest.mjs` creates the compact `content/attire-images.ts` lookup without shipping prompts to the browser. The original supplied poster is retained at `docs/design/bhusha-reference.png` for comparison.

### Additional Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh illustrations

The user separately requested generated Bhusha images for Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. The additional images use the existing portrait framing and warm ivory background, with complete outfits, and are rendered using `object-fit: contain`. Their data are separate from the 28 numbered poster entries; the original manifest and `culturalStates` inventory remain unchanged.

- **Jammu and Kashmir (`IN-JK`):** a Kashmiri attire example showing a man's woollen pheran and trousers and a woman's embroidered pheran and headscarf. [Press Information Bureau's account of traditional Kashmiri clothing](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=116916) supports the pheran and related coverings. This is a Kashmiri example, not a claim to represent every regional or community tradition in Jammu and Kashmir.
- **Ladakh (`IN-LA`):** a ceremonial attire example showing a man's goncha robe and waist sash and a woman's sulma dress and turquoise perak headdress. [Monisha Ahmed's field-research account, “Ladakhi Dress and Costume”](https://www.sahapedia.org/ladakhi-dress-and-costume), supplies the clothing context. The perak is ceremonial and especially associated with Buddhist married women; it is not presented as universal daily dress.

Both entries are visibly labelled AI-generated illustrations informed by cultural references, not original photographs or entries from the supplied Bhusha poster. Their source links, including [Bowers Museum's perak account](https://www.bowers.org/index.php/collection/collections-blog/snakes-and-stones-perak-headdresses-from-ladakh), are also listed on `/references#jivan-shaili-credits`. Exact generation prompts and provenance are retained in `docs/design/attire-generated-territories.json`. No new native-script clothing labels were inferred for either territory. Their subsequently requested language lists use distinct `languageSources` references and the same horizontal, wrapping Bhasha display as the states.

### Additional Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh language lists

- **Jammu and Kashmir:** Kashmiri, Dogri, Gojri, Pahari, Punjabi, Urdu and Hindi. [District Udhampur's language overview](https://udhampur.nic.in/language/) and [IGNCA's multilingual Bhasha Parv programme](https://ignca.gov.in/hi/events/bhasha-parv-multilingual-kavi-sammelan-on-languages-and-dialects-of-jk/) provide evidence of regional usage. The programme uses the spelling “Pahadi”; the display uses the common variant “Pahari”.
- **Ladakh:** Ladakhi, Purgi, Balti and Shina. [The Ladakh administration's regional-language course announcement](https://ladakh.gov.in/gdc-kargil-introduces-ability-enhancement-courses-in-regional-languages-of-ladakh/) and [India Cine Hub's Ladakh overview](https://www.indiacinehub.gov.in/location/states-union-territories/ladakh) support these regional examples. Bhoti is not added or equated with Ladakhi because the consulted naming categories do not establish an exact equivalence.

These are non-exhaustive examples of regional languages and varieties, not official-language designations or rankings by number of speakers. Language references are stored separately from attire sources, and no native-script forms have been invented.
